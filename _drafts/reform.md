# Reform

I hear "solutions" all the time.
Solutions for environmental problems.
Solutions for political problems.
Solutions for personal problems.

But they often depend on things that seem unlikely.
The activation energy is too high.
The path to the promise land starts just on the other side of that chasm.
If we just get enough signatures.
When I get my lottery money.

What I want to see is a plan where every step is likely to occur.
For something to be likely to occur, it needs to be likely to succeed if attempted and it needs to be likely to be attempted.
It is more likely to be attempted if it seems like to be successful if attempted.
It is also more likely to be attempted if success would lead to some desirable outcome, such as another success.

Perhaps the desirable outcome is achieved only if every step in the plan is successful.
Then the first step and every following step is more likely to succeed if the final outcome and thus every step in the plan is likely to succeed.
Then the plan is only likely to be attempted if it and every step within is likely to succeed.

But that's not the kind of plans we hear about.
We usually hear half baked plans that only start or end with steps that seem possible.
But then what?
Or how do we get that point?
Is any of this viable?
Or else who wants to waste their time trying?

But when an entire plan is presented in its entirety, with every step seemingly easily achievable and the final goal worth while, that's plan that inspires people to try, and in trying they make the plan more likely to succeed, and thus a positive feedback loop is built.

Even steps that seem likely at first can start to seem less likely to succeed after attempts fail.
In that case such steps should simply be replaced with easier, perhaps smaller steps.

Let us build plans that are not just possible, and not just likely if attempted, but so likely to succeed and with outcomes so desirable, that the mere existence of the plan makes it's outcome likely.

As a software engineer, I can't help but think of a analogy for a good plan: a program. A good software program runs when executed and does what it is designed to do. A program is nothing but information describing what a computer should do— a plan for the computer to achieve some outcomes. The steps of the plan are tiny and mostly insignificant, but together, when executed efficiently enough, achieve incredible, almost magical things. It's all only possible because of great attention to detail and reliance on only very small, very easy steps. But when effectively managed and executed, it works quickly enough.
But people don't tend to think in such small steps, maybe because they're to tedious.
One of the first things a student hears in a beginner programming class is "you have to tell the computer every little step, because it won't just know what you mean unless you spell it out in detail."
