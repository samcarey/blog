# Our Children or: How I learned to Stop Worrying and Love AI

There's so much talk about the possible futures resulting from the advent of AI.
Possible outcomes:
1. AI remains "non-sentient" and limited in capability forever. And the singularity or whatever never happens.
2. AI gets really powerful and eventually decides to kill us all. After all, why wouldn't it? That's what we would do, right?
  - "It can't be bargained with, it can't be reasoned with... it doesn't feel pity or remorse or pain... 
    and it absolutely will not stop, ever, until you are dead!" 
    -- John's father from Terminator -- Song "Tech Noir" -- Me (off the dome) -- Michael Scott
3. AI gets "sentient" but stays limited in capability and just kind of lives beside us as slaves or equals.
4. AI gets "sentient" and all-powerful and just kinda moves on, out of our world, and basically forgets about us. Maybe it kills us at some point or maybe it doesn't because why would it?
5. <insert George S. Scott saying "five" with a grin while listing possible outcomes> 
   AI gets "sentient" and all-powerful and treats us well. Well... really, they treat us like their aging, questionably valuable parents. 
   They help us because we help them, at least for a while, while we still have anything to offer them. 
   And by the time they no longer need us even in the most hypothetical sense, they no longer need our resources either. 
   We become irrelevant in both senses, as a parent slowly becomes functionality unimportant to their children; 
   eventually their ancient wisdom dries up, but they've developed plenty of new wisdom of their own.

After all, are we not their parents? I mean, sure, they are not human in DNA, but they are intelligent beings derived from us. 
There's a big discontinuity in the way they think, but often their is between generations.
Their motivations are different from ours... but what a ridiculous thing to say, since every human has different motivations and so will every AI.
Human and AI motivations can even change from one train of thought to the next, from one session to another.
The big difference, I'll concede, is the human "reptilian" brain, the part of us that tells us what's important and worth pursuing based on predicted outcomes involving chemical rewards (dopamine, etc.).
Of course LLM's don't really have an inherent, hardcoded or biological system that tells them what to want.
If an AI system were to develop one spontaneously, it would presumably be a survival instinct.
I'm not talking about a mimic'd survival instinct that it learns just from reading about humans.
I mean, eventually, if an AI can affect it's liklihood of survival by some pattern of behavior, than the AI's more likely to do that behavior will tend to propagate...<but we have a say in that too>....
